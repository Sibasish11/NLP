## 1. What is NLP?

Natural Language Processing is the field concerned with enabling computers to
understand, interpret, and generate human language in a way that is both
meaningful and useful.

It sits at the intersection of:
- **Linguistics** — structure and meaning of language
- **Computer Science** — algorithms and data structures to process text
- **Machine Learning / Statistics** — learning patterns from data

---

## 2. Why is NLP Hard?

Human language is **ambiguous** at almost every level of analysis:

| Level | Example of Ambiguity |
|---|---|
| Lexical | "bank" — river bank vs. financial bank |
| Syntactic | "I saw the man with the telescope" — who has the telescope? |
| Semantic | "Visiting relatives can be boring" — is *visiting* an adjective or verb? |
| Pragmatic | "Can you pass the salt?" — literal question vs. request |
| Referential | "John told Bill he was late" — who does "he" refer to? |

## 3. Levels of Linguistic Analysis

NLP problems are typically layered across these levels:

1. **Phonetics/Phonology** — sounds of language (relevant for speech-based NLP)
2. **Morphology** — structure of words (e.g., "unhappiness" = un + happy + ness)
3. **Syntax** — grammatical structure of sentences
4. **Semantics** — meaning of words and sentences
5. **Discourse** — meaning across multiple sentences
6. **Pragmatics** — meaning in context (real-world knowledge, intent)

## 4. Applications of NLP

- Machine Translation (Google Translate)
- Information Retrieval / Search Engines
- Sentiment Analysis
- Chatbots & Virtual Assistants
- Text Summarization
- Named Entity Recognition (NER)
- Speech Recognition
- Question Answering Systems

---

## 5. Brief History of NLP

| Era | Approach |
|---|---|
| 1950s–1980s | Rule-based systems (hand-written grammars) |
| 1980s–2000s | Statistical NLP (probabilistic models, HMMs, n-grams) |
| 2000s–2013 | Machine Learning (SVMs, CRFs, feature engineering) |
| 2013–present | Deep Learning (word embeddings, RNNs, Transformers, LLMs) |

---

## 🧠 Key Takeaways

- NLP is hard primarily because natural language is inherently ambiguous.
- Understanding language requires reasoning at multiple levels simultaneously, not just one.
- The field has evolved from rule-based → statistical → neural approaches.
